# 🌾 Farm Empire: The Jevons Paradox 🌾

An interactive educational game that teaches the Jevons Paradox through agricultural economics.

## 🎯 What is the Jevons Paradox?

The **Jevons Paradox** (named after economist William Stanley Jevons) states that as technological improvements increase the efficiency with which a resource is used, the total consumption of that resource may actually **increase** rather than decrease.

### In Agriculture:

1. **Better Technology** → More crops per acre (higher productivity)
2. **Higher Productivity** → More profit per acre
3. **More Profit** → Greater incentive to farm MORE land
4. **Result**: Total land use **INCREASES** even though you could produce the same amount on less land!

## 🎮 How to Play

### Getting Started

1. Open `index.html` in any modern web browser
2. You start with:
   - $100 in money
   - 3 farm plots
   - 1x productivity (baseline technology)

### Game Mechanics

**🌽 Next Turn (Harvest & Sell)**
- Harvests crops from all your land
- Crops = Land Plots × 10 crops/plot × Productivity Level
- Sells crops for $5 each
- Advances to the next turn

**🏞️ Buy Land Plot**
- Purchase new farmland to expand your operation
- Cost starts at $50 and increases with each purchase
- Maximum 64 plots available

**🔬 Upgrade Technology**
- Improves farming productivity by 0.5x per upgrade
- Cost starts at $100 and increases with each upgrade
- Makes each plot produce more crops

### Experiencing the Paradox

1. **Phase 1 - Starting Out**
   - Play a few turns with your initial 3 plots
   - Notice your production and income
   - This is your baseline

2. **Phase 2 - Upgrade Technology**
   - Save money to upgrade your farming technology
   - Notice how each plot now produces MORE crops
   - Your profit per plot increases significantly

3. **Phase 3 - The Paradox Emerges**
   - Even though you COULD produce your original amount on fewer plots...
   - It becomes economically attractive to buy MORE land!
   - Each new plot is highly profitable because of your improved technology
   - Watch your total land use INCREASE

4. **Phase 4 - Observation**
   - Check the "Jevons Paradox Alert" panel (appears when paradox occurs)
   - Review the "Land Use Over Time" graph
   - Read the insights showing how efficiency led to expansion

## 📊 Understanding Your Dashboard

### Stats Panel
- **Money**: Your available funds
- **Total Production**: Crops produced per turn
- **Productivity Level**: Technology multiplier
- **Land Plots Owned**: Total farmland
- **Turn**: Current turn number

### Farm Grid
- **Green plots with 🌾**: Your active farmland
- **Gray plots**: Available land to purchase
- Watch plots sway as they grow crops!

### Land Use Graph
- Tracks your land ownership over time
- Shows the expansion pattern
- Visual proof of the paradox in action

### Insights Panel
- Real-time analysis of your decisions
- Shows how much land you "need" vs. how much you own
- Explains why you're expanding despite efficiency gains

## 🎓 Educational Objectives

This game demonstrates:

1. **Economic Incentives**: How profitability drives decision-making
2. **Rebound Effects**: Why efficiency doesn't always reduce consumption
3. **Historical Patterns**: Mirrors real agricultural expansion despite technological improvements
4. **Critical Thinking**: Challenges intuition about efficiency and conservation

## 🌍 Real-World Examples

### Historical Agricultural Context
- **1960-2010**: Global crop yields increased ~150-200%
- **Same period**: Agricultural land use EXPANDED by ~12%
- **Why?** Higher productivity made farming more profitable globally
- **Result**: More land converted to agriculture, not less

### Other Examples of Jevons Paradox
- **Fuel Efficiency**: More efficient cars → more driving
- **LED Lighting**: Cheaper light → more lights used
- **Data Storage**: Cheaper storage → more data stored
- **Internet Speed**: Faster connections → more data consumption

## 💡 Discussion Questions for Students

1. Why don't farmers reduce their land use after improving productivity?
2. What would need to change to prevent the paradox? (e.g., land use regulations, profit caps)
3. How does the Jevons Paradox relate to environmental sustainability?
4. Can you think of modern technology examples where efficiency led to MORE consumption?
5. Is the Jevons Paradox always a bad thing? When might increased consumption be beneficial?
